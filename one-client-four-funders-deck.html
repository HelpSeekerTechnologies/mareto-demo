02 · Why the numbers differ

The difference is in the rule

None of them needs a different fact. Each needs a different rule applied to the same fact.

1
DefinitionWhat counts as "housed"? One funder counts any move to a rental. Another counts it only if the person is still there six months later.
2
UnitA person, a household, a stay or a visit. A family of four is one household, four people and possibly eight stays.
3
PeriodCalendar quarter, April to March, a September grant year, or "everyone ever served". Point in time or cumulative.
4
GroupingYouth is 16 to 24 for one funder and 18 to 29 for another. Population categories that do not line up.
You asked: track the family, or the individual?Candace House, October 6. Both, from the same records. Record each person, link them to a household, and let the report decide which one it counts.

03 · The report-once model

Capture the fact, derive the answer

Store what happened, when, and to whom. Let each funder's rule do the counting.

Record the factNot the answer

Store the answer and you can answer one funder. Store the fact and you can answer all of them, including the one you do not have yet.
03 · The report-once model

Five parts to reporting once

In the order the work happens. Each works on paper first. A system makes it automatic.

PartWhat you doWhat a system does
1Record onceEach person and each piece of work, recorded once by the person doing it. Facts, not answers.One record per person, shared by every program.
2Write the agreementEach funder written down once: what it pays for, its year, its due dates, its definitions.A funder agreement on file for each program it pays for.
3Let the rule countEach indicator is a rule applied to the facts. Periods are filters, not spreadsheets.Every number on the funder's own year, saying how it was counted.
4Measure honestlyTargets set once. Actuals come from the records. A blank stays a blank.Outcomes against targets: met, on track, not met, not yet measured.
5Close the loopFind the records missing a fact. Share counts, never names.The gaps listed to fix, and a logged export with small counts withheld.

Part 1 · Record once Tool two

Five questions before any new field

Ask them every time a funder, a board or a manager wants one more box on the form.

1 Record once2 Write the agreement3 Let the rule count4 Measure honestly5 Close the loop
1Which report line uses it?If none, it does not go on the form.
2Is it a fact, or an answer?Record the fact. Work the answer out.
3Do we already have it somewhere?Same fact, different label, is still one field.
4Who records it, at what moment?If no one does it during the work, it gets filled in from memory.
5What happens if it is blank?If a blank breaks a report, make it required and define it.
The best reporting systems have fewer fields than you expect, captured at the right moment.

04 · In practice

When it gets messy

Four situations every multi-funder team meets, and the move for each.

A funder requires its own systemKeep your own records as the source. Carry the figures across, and reconcile the two counts each quarter.
A new template arrives mid-yearMap each new line to your crosswalk first. Most are facts you already hold, under a new rule.
Every funder on a different yearOne set of records. Each funder's report reads it on that funder's own year.
A funder asks for namesCheck the agreement and the person's consent first. Counts are the default, and small counts are withheld.
None of these needs a new field. Each one is a rule applied to facts you already keep.
04 · In practice

Four moves for funders and planners

Each costs a funder very little, and saves hours in every agency it funds.

1
Ask for what you useRead last year's template. Drop every line nobody looked at.
2
Publish definitions onceUse shared definitions where they exist. Put your rules in writing, with examples.
3
Align periods and formatsAccept a standard extract. Line up with the periods your co-funders already use.
4
Give the data backReturn comparisons to the agencies that reported. Data that comes back gets better.
Add it up across agencies, counted, never namedWhen funded agencies capture the same core facts by the same definitions, a planner can see the whole system in aggregate. No one shares a name.
04 · In practice

Where does a small team start?

Four steps, in order. All of them work in a spreadsheet.

"Several funders who all count differently. Where should a small team start, and how do we tie our stories to the numbers?"Colchester Food Network, Nova Scotia
1List every report you owe, with its due dateThis week. One sheet, one row per report.
2Mark the facts three or more funders ask forThis month. That short list is your core.
3Define those once and make them requiredBefore next quarter. One line each, with the source.
4Run one quarter from them, then fix the gapsNext quarter end. Note every line you could not fill.
A story is a record tooKeep it on the person's file, with their permission and its purpose, tagged to the goal it shows. Then every number has a story you can point to.
04 · In practice

Choosing a system

Test any system against the five parts, plus three things that keep it yours.

1. One record per personShared by every program. Nobody enters the same person twice.
2. Funder agreementsEach funder's programs, year, due dates and targets, set up once.
3. Every number says its ruleOn the funder's own year, without a spreadsheet.
4. Honest outcomesActuals from records. Blanks stay blank.
5. Gaps and safe exportsThe records to fix, small counts withheld, every file logged.
You own the recordsYou decide who sees what, and you can take all of it out.
Consent travels with the recordWho it may be shared with, and for what.
You can change it yourselfA new form or report line without a developer.
The test: bring last quarter's real reportAsk the vendor to rebuild it from records while you watch, not from their own demo report.
